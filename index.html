The process

How adopting works

It's not complicated, but we do take it seriously. These rabbits have had one home their whole lives and we'd like their next one to be their last.

  1. 1

    Fill in the form

    It's longer than you might expect. That's deliberate — it tells us whether a rabbit will be safe and happy with you.

  2. 2

    We'll get in touch

    Usually within a few days. We may have follow-up questions, and you'll certainly have some for us.

  3. 3

    A chat and a look at the space

    A video call is fine. We just want to see where the rabbit would live and know it's secure and big enough.

  4. 4

    Come and meet them

    Nothing is decided until you've met. Sometimes a different rabbit turns out to be the right one.

  5. 5

    Going home

    We'll send you their history, vet records and the food they're used to, and we'd love the occasional photo.

Before you apply

What a rabbit actually needs

Rabbits are often bought as easy starter pets. They aren't. Please have a read — if any of this is a surprise, it's much better to find out now.

📐 Real space

The recommended minimum is a permanently accessible enclosure of about 3m × 2m × 1m, day and night. A traditional hutch on its own is nowhere near enough — a rabbit should be able to take three hops in a row and stand fully upright on its back legs.

🐇 Company

Rabbits are social and are miserable alone. Ideally they live with another neutered rabbit. If you already have one, we can help with bonding — but it takes weeks of patience, not an afternoon.

🌾 The right food

Unlimited good hay is around 85% of the diet, plus a handful of fresh greens and a small measured portion of pellets. Muesli mixes cause serious dental and gut disease.

🩺 Vet costs

Annual vaccinations, and a rabbit-savvy vet — not every practice is. Rabbits hide illness until they're very unwell, so an emergency appointment can be needed at short notice. Insurance or savings genuinely matter.

🔒 Security

Foxes, cats, birds of prey and dogs. Outdoor runs need a solid roof, a dig-proof base and secure bolts. Indoors, cables need protecting — from the rabbit as much as for it.

⏳ Years, not months

A well-cared-for house rabbit lives 8–12 years. Please think about university, moving, new babies and new jobs before you apply — not after.
