Before you apply

What a rabbit actually needs

Rabbits are often bought as easy starter pets. They aren't. Please have a read — if any of this is a surprise, it's much better to find out now.

📐 Real space

The recommended minimum is a permanently accessible enclosure of about 3m × 2m × 1m, day and night. A traditional hutch on its own is nowhere near enough — a rabbit should be able to take three hops in a row and stand fully upright on its back legs.

🐇 Company

Rabbits are social and are miserable alone. Ideally they live with another neutered rabbit — which, since ours aren't yet, is worth reading the note below about. Bonding takes weeks of patience, not an afternoon.

🌾 The right food

Unlimited good hay is around 85% of the diet, plus a handful of fresh greens and a small measured portion of pellets. Muesli mixes cause serious dental and gut disease.

🩺 Vet costs

Annual vaccinations, and a rabbit-savvy vet — not every practice is. Rabbits hide illness until they're very unwell, so an emergency appointment can be needed at short notice. Insurance or savings genuinely matter.

🔒 Security

Foxes, cats, birds of prey and dogs. Outdoor runs need a solid roof, a dig-proof base and secure bolts. Indoors, cables need protecting — from the rabbit as much as for it.

⏳ Years, not months

A well-cared-for house rabbit lives 8–12 years. Please think about university, moving, new babies and new jobs before you apply — not after.

✂️ None of them are neutered — please read this bit

We're being upfront because it genuinely matters. All ten are entire, and two of them have had a litter already, so this is not theoretical.

  • An unneutered male and female together will breed. If you take two, they must either be the same sex or be neutered first — otherwise you will very quickly have more rabbits than you planned on.
  • Rabbits usually can't be bonded until they're neutered. Entire rabbits tend to fight, sometimes badly. So the company they need really does depend on it.
  • Unspayed females are at high risk of uterine cancer as they get older. It's one of the main reasons vets recommend spaying does.
  • It costs money. Expect somewhere around £80–£150 per rabbit in the UK, depending on the practice and whether it's a male or a female. Please factor it in before you apply rather than after.

We're not asking you to promise anything on the spot — just to go in with your eyes open. If you'd like to talk it through, please do ask.

🧰 What comes with each rabbit

So that nobody has to buy everything at once, each rabbit goes to their new home with:

  • A carrier for the journey
  • A water bottle
  • A starter bag of the food they're used to, so you can change them over gradually rather than all at once — a sudden diet change upsets a rabbit's gut
  • A hutch — honestly described: it's old, it will need some repairs, and it's a starting point rather than a permanent home. It will get you going while you sort out something bigger.
